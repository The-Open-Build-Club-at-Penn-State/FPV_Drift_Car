# Contribution Guidelines

## General Guidelines

The overarching goal of this section is to make sure that the documentation and design remain cohesive, giving the illusion that it was made by an organized group of individuals even when the work is really being done by a disjoint group of collaborators. In general, contributions should follow the same style that the rest of the documentation follows, and breaches in style should first be green-lit by the project's maintainers before a submission is made. A general understanding of the parts and systems that are to be modified or added to is necessary to produce good contributions, so please read the relevant documents before beginning work on the project.

## Project-Specific Guidelines

One of the main design philosophies with this car is that it should be as beginner-friendly as possible, so contributions generally should not add additional complexity onto the existing design. Introducing new tools, exotic materials, and atypical manufacturing methods should therefore be avoided. Anticipate the average maker of this design to be somebody with little to no experience in anything engineering-related.

# Documentation Structure

The documentation is broken into three main documents: The README (what you are currently reading), the Design Document, and the Production Document.

The Design Document contains information that explains and justifies the final design. Reading it is not required to manufacture the design, and so the purpose of it is to answer any questions that may arise in the construction of the design. An added benefit of the Design Document is that it eases the contribution process by explaining the design philosophy (and possibly the shortcomings) of the existing parts.

The Production Document contains all of the information necessary for the production of the part, including the Bill of Materials, Necessary Equipment, and a step-by-step guide on how to produce each part and assembly. Engineering drawings and supplementary images/videos will be provided where appropriate.

# What does Open Source mean?

The Open Source Movement seeks to make information free ("free" as in free speech, not free beer). Traditionally, "information" has narrowly meant software, but in recent years this has expanded to also include hardware schematics.

There have been many different organizations that have adhered to and developed the Open Source Movement, and there exist very authoritative sources on what the movement means and (just as importantly) doesn't mean. The Free Software Foundation's GNU Project defines Free Software in the "Philosophy" section of their website as follows:

> Specifically, free software means users have the four essential freedoms: (0) to run the program, (1) to study and change the program in source code form, (2) to redistribute exact copies, and (3) to distribute modified versions.

It is important to bring up the Free Software Movement specifically as it is a very mature and established movement, and as such the related Free Hardware movement inherits many of its tenets from it. However, there are some key differences between Free and Open Source Software and Free and Open Source Hardware. The GNU Project, on the same webpage, also argues why their purview is specific to software:

> Software differs from material objects—such as chairs, sandwiches, and gasoline—in that it can be copied and changed much more easily. These facilities are why software is useful; we believe a program's users should be free to take advantage of them, not solely its developer.

While this view is not necessarily outdated, it is certainly a noble goal to try and make it so. Whether they be called "Hackspaces" or "Makerspaces," there is a growing contingency of people who feel the need to be in control of their products—not the other way around. We don't believe that this movement is contrary to the goals of the Free Software Movement, but rather a complementary expansion of the movement. While the barrier for average people to manufacture their own eyeglasses or computer chips is clearly insurmountable for the foreseeable future, it is clear that giving the general population more alternatives and more control over their material products will increase their freedom.

# What is The Open Build Club?

Open Build Club is a recognized student organization of the Pennsylvania State University.

The Open Build Club is an organization founded in 2026, and is based on the values of the Open Source movement. It is our club's goal to create an environment where undergraduate engineers can not only grow and develop their skills, but also create contributions to the greater engineering community and benefit as many people as possible with their work. We are a multi-disciplinary club with free-forever membership.
